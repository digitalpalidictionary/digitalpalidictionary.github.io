    Pāḷi Resources

    Courses, reading apps, dictionaries and study tools for students of Pāḷi. Tick the devices you own to see only what runs on all of them. Websites work on any device, so they are always included. Tick Recommended to see only the resources we recommend. Software made for one of your devices is listed first, ahead of anything that reaches it through a website.

    Works on all of:

    Reading the Tipiṭaka

    The first nine have DPD built in, so every word is one tap from a definition.

    ePitaka: the full Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana Tipiṭaka with commentaries, side by side with translations in over 20 languages. It carries the complete DPD, with the deconstructor and grammar dictionary, and works offline. It also runs in a browser at epitaka.org. Read DPD in ePitaka.

    TipitakaPali.org: a simple reader that runs in a browser or installs as an offline app on every platform, with DPD, the PTS Pāḷi-English Dictionary and Monier-Williams on every word. Read DPD in TipitakaPali.org.

    Tipitaka Pali Reader: a reader that uses DPD's own inflection and sandhi recognition, so its popups show the same analysis as DPD. Read DPD in Tipitaka Pali Reader.

    Simsapa Dhamma Reader: a desktop study library with the Tipiṭaka, SuttaCentral texts, a full set of Pāḷi and Sanskrit dictionaries and Dhamma e-books. It updates itself with each new DPD release. Read DPD in Simsapa.

    SuttaCentral: the texts of every early Buddhist school, with translations in many languages. It uses a light version of DPD for English word lookup. Read DPD in SuttaCentral.

    The Buddha's Words: English on the left and Pāḷi on the right, with a DPD definition on hover. The whole site downloads for offline reading through Kiwix. Read DPD in The Buddha's Words.

    PaliVerse: the root texts and commentaries in one consistent English translation, with a DPD card on every Pāḷi word. It runs in a browser and needs no account. Read DPD in PaliVerse.

    sutamaya: a quiet sutta reader for phones and the browser, with translations by Bhikkhu Sujato, Bhikkhu Bodhi and Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu. It works offline from the first launch. Read DPD in sutamaya.

    Dhamma.gift: a search engine for the suttas with filtering and grouping, plus browser extensions, an Android app, a Telegram bot and a PWA, all with DPD lookup. Read DPD in Dhamma.gift.

    Digital Pāḷi Reader: opens a sutta together with its commentary, sub-commentary and translation, with Pāḷi-English dictionaries on every word and a built-in quiz. It works in many scripts and can be installed as an offline app.

    The Pāḷi Tipiṭaka: the authenticated Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana texts published by the Vipassana Research Institute. This is the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana edition, the source of DPD's CST references.

    Access to Insight: a large library of English translations of suttas, with introductions and articles from the Buddhist Publication Society and the Thai Forest tradition. The whole website can be downloaded for offline reading.

    Dhammatalks.org: Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu's translation of the entire Sutta Piṭaka, available as a hyperlinked e-book for offline reading, with free Dhamma e-books from the Thai Forest tradition.

    Reading Faithfully: a guide to building a daily sutta-reading habit, with an overview of the different translators. Its Sutta Finder finds a discourse by name.

    Palikanon.com: the German translation of the Pāḷi Canon, with a Pāḷi handbook, a Pāḷi-German dictionary and a dictionary of proper names. The main resource for German readers.

    Bibliotheca Polyglotta: the University of Oslo's library of parallel texts, publishing the Pāḷi Tipiṭaka alongside English and Thai translations.

    Pāḷi Dictionaries

    Digital Pāḷi Dictionary — install DPD on Android, iPhone and iPad, Windows, Mac or Linux, or open DPD Online in any browser. There are e-readers too: Kindle, Kobo and KOReader.

    Offline dictionary packs: the PTS and DPPN dictionaries, and many more, packaged for offline use on every device.

    A Dictionary of Pāli by Margaret Cone: the modern scholarly dictionary from the Pāḷi Text Society, published in three parts. Searchable on Gandhari.org.

    Critical Pāli Dictionary (CPD): the scholarly dictionary of the canon compiled for the Royal Danish Academy from 1925 to 2010, with concordances and bibliography. Hosted online by the University of Cologne.

    PTS Pāḷi-English Dictionary (PED) by Rhys Davids and Stede: the 1921–25 classic that every other dictionary builds on, searchable at the University of Chicago. For a faster lookup with wildcard search, use the Reading Faithfully edition.

    Pāḷi Dictionary: one search box for Pāḷi dictionaries in English, Japanese, Chinese, Vietnamese and Burmese. The only multilingual dictionary here.

    Dictionary of Pāli Proper Names (DPPN) by G. P. Malalasekera, revised by Ven. Ānandajoti: the people and places of the Pāḷi texts, in a revised edition that is also available for offline use.

    Nyanatiloka's Buddhist Dictionary: a manual of Buddhist terms and doctrines that gives each term's Sanskrit equivalent and cross-references to the texts.

    Wisdom Library: definitions of Buddhist, Hindu and Jain terms gathered from many dictionaries and texts.

    Sanskrit and Related Dictionaries

    Pāḷi words are often easier to understand beside their Sanskrit relatives.

    Cologne Digital Sanskrit Dictionaries: more than 40 Sanskrit dictionaries, including Monier-Williams, Apte and Edgerton's Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit Dictionary.

    The Sanskrit Heritage Site: Gérard Huet's Sanskrit-French dictionary and an edition of Monier-Williams, both linked to grammatical tools such as inflection tables.

    Digital Dictionaries of South Asia: the University of Chicago's dictionary library, with the PTS Pāḷi-English Dictionary and the Sanskrit dictionaries of Apte, Cappeller and Macdonell.

    A Dictionary of Gāndhārī: the dictionary of the Gāndhārī language, on the same site as Cone's Dictionary of Pāli, so the two can be compared. The site also searches Monier-Williams and Edgerton's Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit Dictionary.

    Digital Dictionary of Buddhism (DDB): about 80,000 entries on Buddhist terms in English, Sanskrit, Pāḷi, Tibetan, Chinese, Japanese and Korean. The place to look for a term that entered Pāḷi studies from East Asia. Non-members can log in as "guest".

    Courses

    DPD Pāḷi Courses: the Beginner Pāḷi Course and the Intermediate Pāḷi Course, with exercises and answer keys. The Beginner Course starts from the alphabet and pronunciation and works up to nouns, verbs and sandhi.

    The Pāli Language: Level 1 and Level 2 from the Open Buddhist University: free, self-paced classes built on Bhikkhu Bodhi's recorded lectures, with handouts and homework. Level 1 works through Lily de Silva's Pāli Primer in 26 lectures, and Level 2 through Gair and Karunatillake's A New Course in Reading Pāli.

    A Course in the Pali Language by Bhikkhu Bodhi: the lecture series itself, hosted by Bodhi Monastery with the handouts. It teaches grammar and vocabulary through direct study of selections from the Buddha's discourses.

    OCBS Online Courses from the Oxford Centre for Buddhist Studies: Pāḷi courses taught by Prof. Richard Gombrich and Dr Alexander Wynne, with group access for monasteries and institutions. Enrolment goes through their course shop.

    Study Tools and Reference

    The OCBS Pali Hub: full bibliographies of Pāḷi studies worldwide and the Gombrich archive, from the Oxford Centre for Buddhist Studies. The place to look for academic editions and research on a text.

    Pali Text Society: founded in 1881 to foster the study of Pāḷi texts. Its shop lists the canonical texts in Pāḷi, English translations, dictionaries and grammars, and the Journal of the Pali Text Society: books and publications.

    Omniglot: Pāḷi: the Pāḷi alphabet in every script it is written in, with pronunciation and sample text.

    Aksharamukha: converts Pāḷi between Roman and the scripts of Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia, Laos, India and Tibet. It is also available as a Python package, and is what DPD uses for script conversion.

    Anki: a DPD vocabulary deck for the Anki flashcard app, on desktop, Android and iOS.
